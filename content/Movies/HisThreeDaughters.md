# Cast:

Rachel: Natasha Lyonne

Christina: Elizabeth Olsen

# Initial Notes
- The very first scene of Katie talking at Rachel sets the tone for their relationship and paints a strong picture of the kind of person Katie is
- Christina does the same thing to Rachel, with the only real difference that Katie is more critical of Rachel than Christina is
- Hospice nurse early on establishing the stakes of the movie: their dad won't die until they're able to reassure him that they're going to be ok without him, and this happens right after the scenes where both Katie and Christina talked at Rachel
- The through line in the movie of Katie being fixated on the DNR and handling the logistics as a way to cope with her dad dying without really facing the emotional aspect of it
- Christina when she's on the phone with her daughter saying she's not crying because she's sad when her dad is dying
- The clear connection between Christina and Katie when they do the thing where they speak gibberish to each other
- Christina talking about her daughter and how much she loves her, then Christina talking about her teenage daughter and how often they're at odds
- Katie and Christina are very Martha and Mary coded, however, Rachel was the one doing the bulk of the caretaking before the two of them swooped in. Feels like there's a bit here about Rachel making herself invisible and just doing the work.
- The way the dad supposedly talked about deadlines during his limited window of coherence, and how you can see that much more in Christina and Katie than in Rachel
- After Katie goes on her monologue to Rachel about smoking in the house and Katie saying that Rachel smoking outside the house is her way of saying "I'm respecting Katie" and the very next shot being Rachel smoking outside. Despite them still being at the point where neither of them are really expressing it towards each other, clear evidence that they do love and care for each other
- Christina talking at Rachel when she's outside smoking and Rachel telling Christina that she's crazy and Rachel being a bit critical of Christina like Katie is of Rachel
- Neither Katie nor Rachel understanding Christina's love of The Grateful Dead, striking because they have so little in common 
- Rachel never entering the room their dad is in when he starts breathing weird while the other two sisters are in there, striking given they keep going on about not wanting to invade Rachel's space
- Hospice nurse repeatedly telling them essentially how to circumvent rules to give their father a death that is better but not quite legal (not calling the EMTs right away to circumvent not having a DNR and telling them they could OD their dad on morphine as long as they say it's accidental), and the girls being horrified even though they were the ones that railed on about how unfair a given law was
- Rachel defending her sisters to Benjy, even though he's the only one that treats Rachel like she's a competent adult
- Even though in theory I wanted to like Benjy sticking up for Rachel to Katie and Christina, there was something that was really grating because she never asked for him to do that, also he projected a lot of stuff on to the two of them like they do to Rachel, except he's an outsider so it's different.
- Very distinct colors of the house, Rachel's room being blue tinted, but the rest of the house being warm colors (living area being orange and yellow, dad's room being red) also the apartment lobby being green and outside when Rachel goes to smoke being nighttime and black
- Katie wanting to leave as soon as she has the big blowout with Rachel, very much not wanting to deal with any kind of deep emotional distress
- Both Rachel and Katie are significantly more delicate with Christina than with each other. 
- When they talk about their relationship with their dad, everyone being near the point of tears but no one actually crying
- Katie apologizing to Christina the following morning when she should be apologizing to Rachel
- After Katie gets on Rachel about bringing someone over they don't know (Benjy), they bring in the DNR nurse and Rachel is blindsided by this actual stranger in the apartment
- Rachel talking at the guy in her weed shop the same way her sisters talk at her.
- Rachel actually speaking up with the hospice nurse and calling him out on saying every day that this could be the last day and that being when things start to turn the corner with Katie
- Rachel immediately leaving when she hears Katie have an argument on the phone with her daughter
- The arizona tea Rachel gives Christina as a peace offering
- The first time Rachel ever enters the room where her dad is (and even then it's just standing right in the threshold), it's to talk about the bets she's made, you can tell it's something she talks to him about a lot and how it's her acknowledging her dad but also not wanting to face that he's dying
- Conversation that Christina has on the phone with her family and you can see how her husband shrugs her off and says he's busy, all of this after she clearly works hard to exude this image of domestic bliss
- When doing the obituary, Katie and Rachel both come up with lists of traits and things about their father, Christina is the one that tells an anecdote
- Christina quoting their dad as saying "The only way to convey death is through absence" and that connecting to not showing the dad up to that point
- The first time Rachel fully enters the room where her dad is dying is when the three of them go in together, and then they pull him out to the living room so he can sit in his recliner. Also striking how this is where they're all working together and laughing
- When dad gives his whole speech to the girls, Katie being more affectionate than she's ever been with her sisters during this movie
- The switch where you realize him talking and all that was some kind of ghost sequence, then makes you question if the bond between those three was also some kind of dream sequence kind of thing. The way the ghost sequence actually makes total sense, but it's still a surprise because you could see a reality where the three daughters were infantalizing him and dosing him up on too much morphine that he wouldn't be coherent
- The whole bit where each of them sit on their dad's recliner as a kind of ritual to start to acknowledge his death
- The three of them all cuddled up next to each other on the couch afterwards
- The cute song that Christina sings (probably normally to her daughter) about the little ducks all going away, and then Rachel at the end adding "daddy duck said beep beep beep and then all three little ducks came back" this humor that was a great way to end the movie
