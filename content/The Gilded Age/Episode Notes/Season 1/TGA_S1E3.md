# Initial Things noted
- Bertha coming in with a newspaper at the beginning at the scene
	- Seeing how involved she is with the business here
- Even though George is known as a Robber Barron and is seen as this dirty new money, it's the aldermen that throw the curveball of rescinding the law they put in place for George building a station in NYC so they can sell the stock high and make money off of George's company
- The certainty Bertha has when she says to George that he's made one fortune and he can make another one
- The way Bertha immediately sees through Oscar and knows he's just after Gladys for her money
- George/Bertha talk a lot more about the business now than later in the series
- George is also a lot more confident now than he will be later
- At dinner, there's foreshadowing about the Met plot in S2
- Mr. Morris ordering his wife to grovel to Mrs. Russell as if that will impact what George is doing
	- Denial that the train has left the station and that this could still be fixed. He doesn't appreciate that this is about the attitudes and not interactions.
- The shot of Bertha swooping in in front of her portrait
	- Depiction of her wealth and power she wants to exude
- Bertha casually making Anne feel like an idiot about business
	- Bertha saying that she wasn't going to do Anne a favor when she's gotten nothing (not playing the game of doing favors and deferring to society just because of their rank in society)
	- Bertha dismissing Anne as a shift from vying to invite the Morrises for dinner 
- Raikes is annoying and a flop
- Agnes seeing right through Eckhard and how he just wants the money he thinks Ada has
	- Even though people think she's heartless, Agnes never tells Ada the truth of Eckhard's intentions. 
	- You can really see how Agnes cares about Ada in these little things that no one really sees
- George actually saying that part of the reason he's buying the stock and doing this thing that puts his fortune in jeopardy is because how this group snubbed his wife. 
	- George coming in with the grandiose gestures of love
